Machine-checked mathematical economics, built on Mathlib.

MathEconLib is a family of Lean 4 libraries that formalize the core results of economic theory, starting with auctions and mechanism design. Every theorem is verified by the Lean kernel and rests on Mathlib, so the definitions compose with the rest of formal mathematics rather than living in a silo.

The library separates three things that textbooks usually blend: the rules of a mechanism, the probabilistic environment it sits in, and the interim optimization problem each agent faces. Keeping those layers apart is what lets a single envelope theorem or payment identity serve auctions, posted prices, and other single-parameter problems without re-proving it each time.

This site is the public face of the project: a stable place to point people at, a home for the documentation, and a record of who works on what. Development happens in the GitHub repositories.

Libraries

auctionlib

The first and currently only library. It formalizes the single-parameter theory of auctions and pricing, from incentive compatibility through revenue equivalence, plus the game-theoretic foundations those results assume. Toolchain: Lean 4.28.0, Mathlib v4.28.0.

Mechanism nallocation rule x and payment rule t over report vectors plus a type environment on (Ω, ℙ) Auction Ω nmechanism together with random types and their distributions fix one bidder and the opponents' strategy BidderProblemone bidder's interim view: win probability, cost, strategy generalized by SingleParameterProblemany affine-in-type quasilinear choice problem, u(v,a) = v·x(a) − t(a)

Incentive compatibility is a typeclass chain, HasUniversalIC ⇒ HasTypeDeviationIC ⇒ HasEnvelopeIC, so a result proved under the weakest notion is available automatically under the stronger ones.

Every result compiles without sorry, and #print axioms on the main theorems shows only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound. Public statements are pinned in ApiStability.lean, so a change to any core theorem breaks the build loudly rather than silently.

Contributing

Contributions are welcome. New theorems in existing chapters, documentation, and applications built on the existing layers can go straight to a pull request. The ForMathlib/ folder, which holds results destined for upstream, is a good first contribution.

Changes to the foundational layers (Mechanism/, BidderProblem/, GameTheory/, Auction/Basic.lean) start with a design issue: sketch the definitions and main statements and get agreement on the shape before writing proofs.

Verification bar for every PR

  • lake build is green.
  • No sorry, admit, or native_decide anywhere in the change.
  • #print axioms on new main results shows only the three standard axioms.
  • Imports regenerated with lake exe mk_all --lib Auctionlib --git.

Roadmap

  • ✓Single-parameter foundations and a deterministic fixed-price buyer problem
  • ✓Measure-first value distributions, seller-profit decompositions, posted-price first-order conditions
  • ✓Mechanism, Auction, and BidderProblem framework; envelope theorem, Myerson's lemma, revenue equivalence
  • ✓All-purchase equilibria, explicit solutions, revenue and welfare analysis, optimal pricing
  • ✓Normal-form games and pure and mixed Nash existence
  • ✓Affiliation, log-supermodularity, finite monotone conditional expectations, finite winner's curse
  • ○Riley–Zeckhauser comparison of posted prices with general selling rules
  • ○Posted-price comparative statics, starting with uniform-distribution parameters
  • ○Continuous affiliation results and the Linkage Principle
  • ○Revelation principle over abstract message spaces
  • ○Extensive-form and obviously strategy-proof mechanisms
  • ○Multi-unit and simultaneous ascending auction models
